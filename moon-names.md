# Moon Names

## Purpose

Star Almanack names Full Moons according to their relationship to the natural astronomical year rather than according to Gregorian calendar months.

The system is derived in part from the seasonal naming convention preserved by the *Maine Farmers' Almanac*, but Star Almanack does not reproduce that system unchanged.

The Maine convention was seasonal rather than simply monthly. Its familiar sequence included:

- Moon After Yule

- Wolf Moon

- Lenten Moon

- Egg Moon

- Milk Moon

- Flower Moon

- Hay Moon

- Grain Moon

- Fruit Moon

- Harvest Moon

- Hunter's Moon

- Moon Before Yule

When a season contained 4 Full Moons, the 3rd was designated the Blue Moon. This allowed the final named Moon of the season to retain its proper seasonal position.

Star Almanack preserves that useful intercalation principle while replacing ecclesiastical and conventional calendar rules with calculated astronomical ones.

## Astronomical Seasons

Star Almanack uses the actual calculated equinoxes and solstices as seasonal boundaries.

The Moon names therefore do not depend upon Gregorian months.

They also do not depend upon ecclesiastical calculations of Lent or Easter.

The astronomical calculation comes first. The name is assigned afterward according to the Moon's relationship to the seasonal structure.

## Star Almanack Moon Names

The ordinary sequence is:

### Winter

1. **Moon After Yule**

2. **Wolf Moon**

3. **Sap Moon**

### Spring

1. **Seed Moon**

2. **Milk Moon**

3. **Flower Moon**

### Summer

1. **Hay Moon**

2. **Grain Moon**

3. **Fruit Moon**

### Autumn

1. **Harvest Moon**

2. **Hunter's Moon**

3. **Moon Before Yule**

Star Almanack substitutes **Sap Moon** for the ecclesiastically associated Lenten Moon and **Seed Moon** for the Egg/Paschal Moon.

Both replacement names are traditional Moon names rather than names invented by Star Almanack.

## Protected Names

Some Moon names describe a specific astronomical relationship and therefore take precedence over an ordinary sequential assignment.

### Harvest Moon

The **Harvest Moon** is the Full Moon nearest the September equinox.

It remains the Harvest Moon whether it occurs before or after the equinox.

### Moon Before Yule

The **Moon Before Yule** is the final Full Moon before the December solstice.

Its name is protected because it describes an astronomical relationship to Yule.

### Moon After Yule

The **Moon After Yule** is the first Full Moon after the December solstice.

Its name is likewise protected.

## Blue Moon

Star Almanack uses the traditional seasonal definition of **Blue Moon**.

When an astronomical season contains 4 Full Moons, the 3rd is designated the:

**Blue Moon**

The ordinary final Moon name of that season remains attached to the 4th Full Moon.

The Blue Moon therefore functions as an intercalary designation. It absorbs an additional lunation without forcing the ordinary seasonal names out of their intended positions.

Star Almanack does not use the later convention in which the second Full Moon occurring within a Gregorian calendar month is called a Blue Moon.

## Frost Moon

Star Almanack normally uses 12 recurring Full Moon names.

**Frost Moon** is a 13th, rarely used name.

It is used when the astronomical arrangement places an additional Full Moon between the Hunter's Moon and the Moon Before Yule.

In such a year, the sequence becomes:

**Harvest Moon → Hunter's Moon → Frost Moon → Moon Before Yule**

Frost Moon is not another form of Blue Moon.

A Blue Moon results from the 4-Full-Moon seasonal intercalation rule.

Frost Moon instead provides a traditional seasonal name for an additional late-autumn lunation when the protected positions of the Harvest Moon and Moon Before Yule require one.

The name **Frost Moon** is traditional. Star Almanack has not invented the name; it assigns the existing name to this specialized position in its astronomical naming system.

## Calculation Principle

Moon names are assigned only after the relevant astronomical events have been calculated.

For each year Star Almanack calculates:

1. the March equinox

2. the June solstice

3. the September equinox

4. the December solstice

5. every Full Moon

The calculated events determine the seasonal structure.

Protected names are then assigned.

The seasonal Blue Moon rule is applied where necessary.

The remaining Full Moons receive their ordinary seasonal names.

This makes the naming system a consequence of the astronomy rather than a property of the Gregorian calendar.
