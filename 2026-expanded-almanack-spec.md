# Expanded Star Almanack — 2026 Scope

## Edition structure

The 2026 Star Almanack SHALL cover the complete ISO 2026 week-numbering year:

- ISO 2026-W01 through ISO 2026-W53
- Monday through Sunday for every week
- Existing zodiac calendar, lunar phases, Wheel-of-the-Year points, and weekly classical-planet ephemerides are preserved

## Fixed-object coverage

The 2026 edition SHALL include a best-visibility entry for every object in these sets:

1. **All Messier objects:** M1 through M110.
2. **All Bayer alpha stars:** every stellar Bayer designation whose Greek letter is alpha (α) in the 88 IAU constellations.
3. **All Bayer beta stars:** every stellar Bayer designation whose Greek letter is beta (β) in the 88 IAU constellations.

Where a Bayer designation has separately designated numbered components (for example α¹/α² or β¹/β²), each separately designated stellar entry is retained rather than silently collapsed.

Named stars keep their established proper names where available, while the Bayer designation remains the stable identity used for completeness checking.

## Best-visibility rule

The preserved Star Almanack observer-first rule remains authoritative:

- best visibility occurs when the object transits the local meridian at 9:00 PM local apparent solar time;
- therefore the apparent solar right ascension satisfies α☉ = αobject − 9h;
- solve for the annual instant when the apparent Sun reaches that right ascension;
- round to the nearest calendar date;
- place the object on that civil date and its corresponding ISO 2026 week date.

## Completeness requirements

The completed source must pass all of the following checks:

- exactly 53 ISO week sections, W01 through W53, with no gaps or duplicates;
- exactly 110 distinct Messier identifiers, M1 through M110;
- every catalogued α Bayer designation included exactly once unless separately numbered components require distinct entries;
- every catalogued β Bayer designation included exactly once unless separately numbered components require distinct entries;
- every fixed object assigned one reproducible best-visibility date;
- preserved 15-case best-visibility regression remains 15/15 PASS;
- existing lunar, zodiac, seasonal, and weekly planetary calculations remain unchanged unless an independently verified correction is required.

## Presentation

The Almanack remains observer-first. Fixed objects are distributed onto the 53 weekly pages by best-visibility date rather than presented as a detached database dump. Each week therefore becomes a practical observing guide while preserving the precise mathematics underneath.
