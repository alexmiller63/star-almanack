# The Most Ordinary Day Throughout the World

The **Most Ordinary Day** is:

**August 9 at 10:53 UTC**

This table shows that single instant as civil time in representative places throughout the world.

The places are deliberately geographically diverse. The Southern Hemisphere is given a slight numerical advantage, and the examples include whole-hour, half-hour, and 45-minute civil-time offsets.

The table demonstrates an important idea: **every entry describes exactly the same instant.**

When it is 10:53 UTC, clocks in Los Angeles read 3:53 AM while clocks in Kathmandu read 4:38 PM. In the Chatham Islands it is already 11:38 PM.

And on Kiritimati, the calendar has already advanced to **August 10**.

Nothing astronomical happened at a different moment in any of these places. Only the civil representation of the moment changed.

That is why Star Almanack gives astronomical events in UTC first.

**One sky. One instant. Many clocks.**
